# Integrated Crop Care, Soil Health, and Post-Harvest Storage Practices
Source: SmartFarm Sustainable Farming Handbook

## Irrigation and Moisture Management
### Drip Irrigation vs. Overhead Watering
Foliar fungal and bacterial pathogens (such as Alternaria, Phytophthora, Septoria, and Xanthomonas) require free moisture on leaf surfaces to germinate and infect plant tissue. Transitioning from overhead sprinklers to drip irrigation delivers water directly to the root zone, keeping leaves dry and reducing foliar disease incidence while conserving 30% to 50% irrigation water.

### Timing of Irrigation
When overhead watering cannot be avoided, irrigate in the early morning hours so sunlight and wind dry the plant canopy rapidly. Avoid late-evening watering, which prolongs overnight leaf wetness and encourages fungal spore germination.

## Soil Fertility, Mulching, and Crop Rotation
### Crop Rotation Principles
Solanaceous crops—including Tomato, Potato, Bell Pepper, and Eggplant—share many soil-borne and residue-borne pathogens. Rotating these plots with non-solanaceous families such as legumes (beans, peas), brassicas (cabbage, cauliflower), or cereals/maize for at least 2 to 3 growing seasons breaks disease cycles naturally.

### Organic Mulching
Applying clean straw, dry grass, or plastic mulch beneath tomato and pepper plants creates a physical barrier between soil-borne fungal spores and lower leaves, stabilizes soil temperature, and reduces moisture evaporation during hot spells above 35°C.

## Post-Harvest Produce Handling and Inventory Storage
### Sorting and Grading
Immediately after harvest, separate bruised, cracked, or disease-spotted produce from healthy grade-A harvests. A single decaying tomato or potato tuber can accelerate ethylene production and secondary bacterial soft rot across an entire storage crate.

### Storage Temperature and Ventilation
Store harvested potatoes in a dark, cool (7°C to 10°C), well-ventilated space to prevent greening (solanine formation) and sprouting. Store mature red tomatoes and bell peppers in clean, ventilated crates away from direct sunlight at moderate cool temperatures (10°C to 13°C) to preserve firmness and shelf life.
