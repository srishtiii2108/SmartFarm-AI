# Potato Plant Disease Identification and Integrated Management Guide
Source: SmartFarm Agronomic Extension Reference (Tuber & Solanaceae Health Manual)

## Potato Early Blight (Alternaria solani)
### Symptoms and Identification
Potato Early Blight causes dark brown, angular to circular lesions with distinct concentric rings on older lower leaves first. Surrounding leaf tissue turns yellow, and lesions are often bounded by major leaf veins. Tubers can also develop dark, sunken, leathery surface lesions during harvest if spores wash into the soil.

### Favorable Conditions and Prevention
Stressed plants (due to nitrogen deficiency, drought, or heavy tuber bulking) are most vulnerable. Preventive measures include planting certified healthy seed tubers, maintaining balanced soil fertility (especially adequate nitrogen and potassium), rotating crops for 2 to 3 years, and allowing tuber skins to mature before harvesting to minimize mechanical bruising.

## Potato Late Blight (Phytophthora infestans)
### Symptoms and Identification
Potato Late Blight is the most critical foliar and tuber disease in potato production. Leaves develop irregular, water-soaked dark green to brown blotches surrounded by a light green halo. During humid mornings, white sporulation is visible along lesion edges on the leaf underside. Infected tubers exhibit reddish-brown granular dry rot extending beneath the skin.

### Favorable Conditions and Prevention
Cool nights (10°C to 15°C), mild days (18°C to 22°C), and relative humidity exceeding 85% create epidemic conditions. Prevention requires planting certified disease-free seed tubers, hilling soil well over developing tubers to block spore wash-down, destroying cull piles and volunteer potatoes, and killing vines at least 2 weeks before harvest.

## Healthy Potato Canopy Management
### Optimal Growing Conditions
Potatoes thrive in loose, well-drained, slightly acidic soil (pH 5.5 to 6.5) with consistent soil moisture during tuber initiation and bulking. Avoid waterlogging, which suffocates roots and promotes bacterial soft rot, and avoid erratic watering that causes hollow heart or growth cracks in tubers.
