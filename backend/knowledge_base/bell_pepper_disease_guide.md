# Bell Pepper Disease Identification and Integrated Management Guide
Source: SmartFarm Agronomic Extension Reference (Capsicum Pathology & Cultivation Guide)

## Bell Pepper Bacterial Spot (Xanthomonas campestris pv. vesicatoria)
### Symptoms and Identification
Bacterial Spot is the most widespread foliar disease of bell peppers. Young leaves develop small, water-soaked spots that enlarge into dark brown or black lesions with pale tan centers, while older leaves often turn yellow and shed prematurely. Defoliation exposes developing pepper pods to sunscald. Fruit lesions appear as raised, rough, wart-like brown scabs.

### Favorable Conditions and Prevention
Warm temperatures (24°C to 30°C), high humidity, and wind-driven rain or overhead irrigation drive rapid infection. Preventive steps include sourcing certified disease-free seed and transplants, planting resistant cultivars, rotating away from peppers and tomatoes for 2 to 3 years, avoiding field work when foliage is wet, and switching to drip irrigation.

## Bell Pepper Fungal & Viral Stress Prevention
### General Leaf Spot and Mosaic Prevention
Cercospora leaf spot (frogeye leaf spot) presents as circular spots with light gray centers and dark brown borders. Viral diseases such as Cucumber Mosaic Virus or Tobacco Mosaic Virus cause mottled light-and-dark green leaf patterns, leaf distortion, and stunted fruit.

### Cultural Best Practices
Maintain balanced calcium and potassium nutrition to prevent blossom-end rot on pepper fruit, sanitize hands and pruning tools with mild soap or approved disinfectant when handling plants, control aphid and whitefly vectors using reflective mulches, and ensure good air circulation between rows.
